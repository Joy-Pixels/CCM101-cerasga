# Mission Reflection

<p align="justify">
  Out of the three cloud platforms I looked at, <b>AWS impressed me the most</b>. What stood out was how many services it offers, over 240 in total. AWS seems to have a tool for almost anything a business might need, from hosting simple websites to building AI applications. Learning AWS is moderate in difficulty, it has a huge amount of documentation and beginner guides, but it also uses many acronyms and unique naming conventions that can be confusing at first. Still, AWS is a great choice if you want the most job opportunities and the widest community support, since it's the most widely used cloud platform today. Since AWS started back in 2006, it has had more time to grow, which is probably why it also has the most data centers around the world, making it a reliable choice for businesses of almost any size.
</p>

<p align="justify">
  Even though AWS, Azure, and GCP are different, they share a lot in common. All three offer the same basic types of services, virtual machines, storage, databases, identity management, and Kubernetes, just with different names. They also all use pay-as-you-go pricing and organize their data centers into Regions and Zones to keep things running smoothly even if something fails.
</p>

<p align="justify">
  This mission taught me that a business's needs should always come first when choosing a cloud provider. A small startup with a tight budget needs something different from a university using Microsoft tools, or a research company that needs powerful computers for AI. Picking the platform that solves the client's actual problem matters more than picking the most popular one.
</p>

<p align="justify">
  Acting like a Cloud Solutions Consultant taught me to think beyond the technical side of things, considering a client's current tools, budget, and future growth. It also showed me that no single platform is "best" overall, each one is good at different things.
</p>

<p align="justify">
  Lastly, my GitHub Portfolio has grown after this mission. I'm getting more used to using it, and the things I learned along the way are helping me improve my portfolio even more. I now have organized notes comparing all three providers, real client recommendations, and a decision matrix, solid proof I can evaluate cloud platforms practically.
</p>
